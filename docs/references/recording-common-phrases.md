# Recording common phrases

These are common phrases that the speaker will say during a class. These phrases can be used to determine timestamps.

## Common phrases kirtan end and class beginning
These mark the end of the kirtan in a combined recording (from the kirtan-splitter examples). There is often no marker at all; then the change from singing to speech is the evidence.
- "Om namo bhagavate vasudeva ajam" - prayer that starts the class right after the kirtan
- "Reading from Srimad Bhagavatam"
- The speaker announcing the verse, e.g. "It's thirty one, first canto" (canto/chapter/verse in any order)

## Common phrases class beginning
- "We are reading from"
- "Reading from"
- "Bhagavad-gita as it is"
- "Srimad Bhagavatam"
- "Om namo bhagavate vasudeva ajam"
- "Sri Caitanya caritamrta"
- "Adi lila"
- "Madhya lila"
- "Antya lila"
- "First canto, chapter nine" - book and verse introduction (canto, chapter and verse numbers vary)
- "Bhagavad-gita as it is, chapter" - book and verse introduction

## Common phrases sections to trim
These start sections that are removed when editing (see editing-standard.md):
- "Om namo bhagavate vasudeva ajam" / "Jaya jaya sri caitanya" - introduction mantra
- "Om ajnana timirandhasya" - oblations, after the purport and before the actual class

## Common phrases class end before questions
- "Any questions?" - The discussclass ends and questions from audience
- "Alright"
- "Questions"
- "Ok, questions" - example: 2012-01-22_KKS_CC-Madhya-20-154_Radhadesh_be.mp3 at 01:00:22

## Common phrases - End of class/recording
- "Srila Prabhupada, ki jaya" - Final end of the recording after the questions

## Exceptions
Recordings during festival and events do not always follow the verse or recording structure. For example during vaisnava holidays like Gaura purnima, Nrsimha caturdasi, Janmastami, Radhastami, Govardhana and other vaisnava days, the speaker might not speak from scripture but either from other stories or memory.

## Languages
The speaker sometimes speaks Dutch, German, Spanish, Hindi or French, and some classes have a live translation. The phrases above can therefore appear in another language, or be followed by their translation. Tools must match them in the recording's actual language.
